How big?
Try one:

How it works: every move transfers from the smaller side to the bigger side — complete tens first, then ones, one unit at a time. Tap a place on the map and the moving cars fly there in one smooth swoop, loop once around it, then carry on to the bigger side. A ten lands on top of the bigger side and the rows below slide down to make room. The ones always sit on the bottom row of each side, lined up with each other, so each one slides straight across to the next free square. The total never changes, so when the smaller side reaches zero, what is left on the bigger side is the answer. The panels keep the order of the problem (first number on the left, second on the right), so the move goes left-to-right or right-to-left depending on which side is smaller.

Voices: after each move only the place that just changed is counted — tens while tens are moving, then ones on their own. The full count is always on screen, and the answer is spoken in full (“The answer is seven tens, three ones!”). The two voices (one per side) take turns. Sound starts after the first tap. You can also open a problem directly, e.g. addition-quest.html?theme=mermaids&a=47&b=26
